Corrections and updates

What our guides got wrong, what we changed after they went up, and how to tell us about a mistake.

How corrections work

When a guide gets a fact wrong, such as a date, an episode count, a name or where a story sits in a franchise, we fix it on the page itself. The checked date at the top of the guide moves forward, the change is listed at the end of that guide, and it goes in the log on this page with the date it was made. Nothing is fixed silently.

If a fix changes what a guide recommends, say a different first entry in a watch order or a pick taken out, the guide says what it used to recommend and why it changed. A pick doesn't come out because a studio or publisher asked: nobody pays to be in a guide, and nobody can pay to be left out of one.

Each entry below is one of four kinds:

  • Correction Something the page said was wrong.
  • Update New information, such as nominees being announced or a release date moving.
  • Added A new section on a page that was already up.
  • Edited Wording a reader might notice, with the facts and picks unchanged.

Typos, broken styling and layout changes aren't listed. The editorial standards explain how facts are checked in the first place.

Send a correction

Email omnitrackr@gmail.com with the address of the page, what it says, and what it should say. A source helps most, and an official one helps more: the studio's, publisher's, network's or award body's own page. A fan wiki or a store listing is still worth sending, and we'll check it against an official source before changing anything.

Spoilers count as mistakes too. If a guide gives away more than it should, tell us where. You won't be named on the page unless you ask to be, and you don't need an account to write in.

Not every message leads to a change. When sources disagree, a guide follows the official one and says that editions or regions differ. Opinions about a pick are welcome too. They can shape the next update of a guide, but they aren't corrections, so they don't go in this log.
