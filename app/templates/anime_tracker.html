Why anime tracking gets complicated

Anime watching often happens across seasons, platforms, recommendations, and long gaps. A show might start as a seasonal watch, pause after three episodes, continue months later, or turn into a favorite after a slow first arc. A streaming queue won't remember any of that.

OmniTrackr treats anime as its own category while still keeping it connected to movies, TV shows, games, music, books, manga, podcasts, and audiobooks. So if you also watch films, play games, and read, a seasonal anime sits in the same library as all of that, and you don't need a separate app for each.

Anime also has unusual tracking problems: cour splits, sequel seasons, movies that continue a series, long-running shows, remakes, and recommendations that depend heavily on genre familiarity. You want enough detail to remember where you were, but logging every episode shouldn't feel like data entry.

A light anime tracking routine

  1. Add shows when you plan to watch them, not only after finishing.
  2. Record year, seasons, and episode count when available so different releases are easier to identify.
  3. Use Update progress on a saved anime to record the last watched episode and, optionally, its season.
  4. Keep Watched unchecked while you are still watching. Updating a checkpoint does not finish the series or change its total episode count.
  5. Rate after you have seen enough to judge the characters, pacing, animation, and ending.
  6. Write review notes about tone, adaptation quality, arcs, comfort-watch value, or who would enjoy it.

That's all on purpose. Save enough to decide what to watch next and leave it there. Tracking anime shouldn't feel like homework.

Seasonal anime and stalled shows

Seasonal anime is easy to start and easy to lose track of. A show may look promising after one episode, sit untouched for weeks, then either become a favorite or drop off the list without you noticing. Tracking where you stopped means you can make that call on purpose.

  • Plan to watch: save the title and leave Watched unchecked. There is no separate planned-status selector.
  • In progress: save your last watched episode with Update progress. For a continuously numbered series, leave season blank; for a special, season 0 is available.
  • Watched: mark finished series clearly so statistics and recommendations stay accurate.
  • Stalled or dropped: keep the last episode and use its Private reminder for a short reason to return or stop. These are your own notes, not additional status options.

For a weekly series, record episode 7 after watching it and add “Start episode 8 next time.” You do not need to rewrite the review each week. Clear progress removes only the episode checkpoint and reminder, preserving your rating, review, and Watched choice.

Writing better anime reviews

The best anime reviews say what watching the show is like. Mention whether the show is slow, episodic, emotionally heavy, action-focused, relaxing, visually inventive, or dependent on prior genre familiarity. If the ending changes your opinion of the whole series, that belongs in the note.

In a public review, don't just list plot points. Tell another viewer whether the show fits their mood, how much patience it asks for, and which tropes or pacing choices might put them off.

When you rate also makes a difference. Some anime shouldn't be rated after one episode, while others make their style clear immediately. If a late arc, ending, animation change, or adaptation choice changes your opinion, update the review instead of leaving the first impression as the final word.

Anime statistics and backlog cleanup

Anime statistics can show how many shows you start versus finish, whether your ratings cluster around certain years, and whether seasonal watching is filling your backlog faster than you clear it. Seeing that makes it easier to drop shows that no longer fit and spend your time on the ones you want to finish.

Statistics say more when anime sits beside the rest of your media. You can compare whether anime is your highest-rated category, whether it has the most unfinished entries, and whether review coverage is lower than it is for movies, books, games, or music.

Privacy and public anime reviews

Not every anime note needs to be public. Private notes can include spoilers, unfinished thoughts, personal context, or messy reactions. Public anime reviews should be written for someone deciding whether to start the show, continue past a slow opening, or avoid something that does not fit their taste.

OmniTrackr lets you choose which reviews are public. Episode checkpoints and their reminders always stay private, including when friends can view the anime category or a review is public. They are included in JSON backups, so keep exported files private. Practice with the anime sample in the demo before changing your own library.
