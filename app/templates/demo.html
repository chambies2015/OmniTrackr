Example Tracking Workflow

A typical OmniTrackr session is intentionally simple. Add the item, choose the right category, mark the current status, add a rating when you have one, and write a note only if it helps future-you. The same rhythm works across categories, so switching from a film to an album or book does not require learning a different app.

  • Before starting: save items as plan-to-watch, plan-to-play, plan-to-read, or similar backlog states.
  • While in progress: track TV seasons, anime episodes, games being played, books being read, and music you are actively revisiting.
  • After finishing: add a rating and a review that explains tone, pacing, difficulty, mood, replay value, reread value, or who might enjoy it.
  • When sharing: keep private notes private and make only polished reviews public when they are useful to other visitors.

What the Demo Helps You Decide

The demo is useful if you are comparing OmniTrackr with spreadsheets, movie-only apps, book shelves, TV trackers, or game backlog tools. Spreadsheets are flexible but require maintenance. Single-category apps can be excellent, but they split your entertainment history into separate places. OmniTrackr is strongest when you want one private library with optional public reviews, friend visibility controls, stats, and exports.

If your tracking habits are simple, the sample collection shows that OmniTrackr can stay lightweight. If your library is larger, the sample statistics and cross-category layout show how the app scales without forcing everything into one generic list.

What This Demo Shows

The demo is meant to answer a simple question: what does OmniTrackr actually do after signup? It shows the app's core value without requiring visitors or AdSense reviewers to create an account.
