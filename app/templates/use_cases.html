What OmniTrackr is for

OmniTrackr is for people who don't stick to one kind of media. In a normal week you might watch a movie and a few TV episodes, keep up with a seasonal anime, play a game, listen to an album, and read a few chapters of a book. Separate apps can handle each of those, but none of them shows you the whole week. OmniTrackr keeps all of it in one place, with the same ratings, reviews, statuses, and statistics across every category.

Tracking shouldn't feel like paperwork. What you get out of it is a history you can search. When someone asks what you watched last winter, which game you never finished, or which albums you rated highest, you can look it up instead of guessing.

Use case 1: a cross-media watchlist and backlog

Most watchlists are tied to one service or category. A streaming app remembers shows on that service. A game store remembers games from that platform. A notes app remembers whatever you typed last. OmniTrackr gives you one backlog for everything you plan to start, everything you're partway through, and everything you've finished.

  • Use Movies and TV Shows for watchlists and completed viewing history.
  • Use Anime for seasonal tracking and long-running series.
  • Use Video Games to separate active games from abandoned or completed ones.
  • Use Books and Music so the slower stuff, and albums you replay, don't get buried under watch history.

Having everything in one backlog also makes it easier to pick what's next. You compare your options on one dashboard instead of scrolling through several apps, and pick by mood, category, or what you haven't finished.

Use case 2: reviews you can reread later

A rating tells you how much you liked something, but not why. An OmniTrackr review is where you write down the rest: who recommended it, what mood you were in, which part stood out, or why you changed the rating after thinking it over. Months later, that note tells you a lot more than the number does.

For public reviews, give enough detail that a stranger can follow your reasoning. Length isn't the point. Mention specific things such as pacing, characters, mechanics, tone, replay value, production, or whether it is a good fit for a certain kind of viewer, player, reader, or listener.

Use case 3: seeing patterns in your taste

Statistics show you your own habits. Once your collection has a few dozen entries, you can see which categories take up most of it, whether you rate generously or harshly, and which media types you keep coming back to. If you want a record of your year but don't want to keep a journal, this covers most of it.

Stats can also make recommendations better. If your highest-rated entries cluster around slow-burn dramas, cozy games, documentary books, or certain anime genres, you have a better idea of what to try next. That clue comes from your own history, which a generic recommendation feed doesn't have.

Use case 4: sharing with friends without oversharing

Friends are the people whose recommendations you trust, because you know their taste. OmniTrackr lets you add friends, see each other's collections as far as privacy settings allow, and keep sensitive or unfinished lists hidden. Taste can be personal: you might share movies and games but keep books, music, or statistics to yourself.

One setup that works well: post finished favorites publicly, share wider collections with friends, and keep in-progress or experimental lists private. Friends see enough to recommend something good, and your whole account doesn't become a public profile.

Use case 5: keeping a portable record

Media platforms change. Services shut down, APIs shift, and accounts can be lost. OmniTrackr has export and import so your library isn't stuck in one place. Exporting your data as JSON gives you a backup you can keep for your own records or use when moving between accounts.

For best results, export after major updates to your collection or after a long tracking session. For most people, one backup a month is enough. If you'd be upset to lose your collection, keep a recent export somewhere safe.

Frequently asked questions

Can OmniTrackr track more than movies?

Yes. OmniTrackr supports movies, TV shows, anime, video games, music, books, manga, podcasts, and audiobooks in one account.

Can I keep some media private?

Yes. Privacy controls let you decide which categories and statistics are visible to friends or public visitors.

Can I export my media collection?

Yes. OmniTrackr includes JSON export and import so you can back up or move your collection data.
