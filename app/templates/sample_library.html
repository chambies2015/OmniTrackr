Why look at a sample library

The records below use real titles, but the reader is made up. Nothing here is copied from a user account or pulled from anyone's private data. The site owner wrote every note on this page to show what a useful entry looks like in each of the nine formats OmniTrackr tracks. To try the controls themselves, open the interactive demo.

Most tracking advice stops at "add your titles". The hard part is what you write next to them. A title alone can't tell you where you stopped, why you stopped, whether you'd recommend it, or who you promised to watch it with. Each record below shows the status, the checkpoint if there is one, and the actual text of the note or review, so you can see how little it takes.

Example records

Nine titles, one per format, in different states: finished, in progress, paused on purpose and waiting on a decision.

Movie

Arrival (2016)

Watched: Yes. Rating: 9/10. Review: Public.

Finished, rated the next morning, and reviewed in a way that would help a stranger decide whether to start it. The review says what the film is like to sit through and leaves the twist alone.

Public review: “A first-contact film that is really about grief and language. Almost nothing explodes; the tension comes from two people trying to understand a written language nobody has seen before. The ending changes how you read everything before it, so it gets better on a second watch. Pick a night when you can give it your full attention.”

TV show

Severance (2022)

Watched: Unchecked. Last watched: Season 2, episode 4. Review: None yet.

Watched with a friend, one or two episodes a week. The checkpoint holds the season and episode, and the private reminder holds the rule that matters most for a shared show.

Private reminder: “Start S2E5. Watching with Sam, don't go ahead.”

Anime

Frieren: Beyond Journey's End (2023)

Watched: Unchecked. Last watched: Episode 17, no season. Review: None yet.

The first season is 28 episodes numbered straight through, so the season stays blank. The reminder marks where a new arc begins, which is the natural place to pause a long show.

Private reminder: “Start 18. The mage exam arc starts here.”

Game

Hades (2020)

Played: Unchecked. Rating: 9/10. Review: Private notes.

Rated after the first escape, because by then the combat and the story had both made their case. It stays unfinished on purpose: the story keeps going after the first clear, and the epilogue takes ten escapes.

Private note: “First clear on run 19, with the bow. Ten escapes for the epilogue. Mark as played when I get there or when I stop wanting to.”

Album

To Pimp a Butterfly, Kendrick Lamar (2015)

Listened: Yes. Rating: 9.5/10. Review: Public.

Rated after three full listens, in order. The review says how to listen as well as whether to, which is often the more useful half for an album.

Public review: “Dense, jazz-heavy and close to 80 minutes long. A poem is pieced together a few lines at a time across the record, and the last track finally reads it all the way through. Don't put this on shuffle.”

Book

Project Hail Mary, Andy Weir (2021)

Read: Unchecked. Last read: Chapter 9. Review: Private, in progress.

Chapters instead of pages, because the reader moves between a paperback and an ebook and only the chapter numbers match in both.

Private note so far: “Funnier than The Martian. The flashback chapters are doing more work than I expected.” Reminder: “Start chapter 10.”

Manga

Vagabond, Takehiko Inoue

Read: Unchecked. Last read: Chapter 150. Review: None yet.

A series that has been on hiatus since 2015, with 37 volumes out. The reminder records a decision, so the title doesn't look forgotten every time the reader scrolls past it.

Private reminder: “Reading slowly on purpose. There may never be an ending.”

Podcast

Serial

Listened: Unchecked. Last listened: Season 1, episode 12. Review: None yet.

The first season is a complete story in twelve episodes. Later seasons follow different cases, so the reminder says that none of them is a cliffhanger waiting to be resolved.

Private reminder: “Season 1 done. The other seasons are separate stories, start any of them.”

Audiobook

The Hobbit, read by Andy Serkis (2020)

Listened: Yes. Rating: 8.5/10. Review: Private notes.

About ten and a half hours. The note is about the performance rather than the book, because the book was already rated years ago on the book shelf.

Private note: “Serkis gives every dwarf a voice you can tell apart, which I never managed on the page. Gollum is exactly who you'd expect.”

What the good notes have in common

None of these notes is long. The private reminders are one line each, and they answer the question you'll have when you come back: where was I, and was I stopping for a reason? "Start S2E5" is more use in three weeks than a paragraph about the episode you just watched.

The two public reviews do something different. They are written for a stranger, so they say what the experience is like (slow, dense, funny, long) and who should wait for another night. Neither one retells the plot. The Arrival review avoids the ending entirely and still tells you the ending matters, which is about as much as a spoiler-free review can say.

The ratings came at different times on purpose. Arrival was rated the morning after, the album after three listens, Hades after the first escape. A score given when the credits roll tends to be a score for the last ten minutes. The movie tracker guide suggests a few anchors for the 0 to 10 scale if you want your ratings to mean the same thing next year.

A weekly cleanup

Once a week, this reader looks at what is unfinished and decides what still appeals. Severance moves on an episode or two. Project Hail Mary's checkpoint goes to the last chapter read. Vagabond doesn't change, and that's fine, because its reminder already explains why. "Paused," "planned" and "playing" describe what the reader intends; they are not extra statuses in the app, and a one-line reminder carries them well enough.

Clear progress is for starting over, for example a second run through Frieren. It removes only the checkpoint and its reminder; the title, the finished choice, the rating and the review stay. Before a big tidy-up, the reader exports a JSON backup, which includes private checkpoints and reminders, and keeps it somewhere private.

The cleanup is also when private notes become public reviews. When Project Hail Mary is finished, the "funnier than The Martian" line will grow into a few sentences about pacing and who the book suits, and the spoilers will stay in the private note.

Privacy choices in the sample

Only Arrival and To Pimp a Butterfly have public reviews, because they're finished and the reviews are free of spoilers. Every other note stays private. Episode, chapter and page checkpoints and their reminders are always private: publishing a review never publishes a checkpoint, so "Watching with Sam" stays between the reader and Sam.

Friend visibility is set by category, separately from public reviews. Letting friends see TV shows shows them Severance and its rating, if there is one, but not the episode checkpoint or the reminder. Copying a public collection or saving a reviewed title doesn't copy anyone's progress either.

What statistics would show

With these nine records, completion statistics count three finished titles (Arrival, the album and the audiobook) and six unfinished ones. A checkpoint at episode 17 or chapter 150 doesn't change the completion count. Four titles are rated, two have public reviews, and review coverage counts the private notes on Hades, the audiobook and Project Hail Mary as written notes. Checkpoint reminders are kept apart from review coverage.

Even at nine titles, the numbers say something: this reader finishes the short things and keeps several long ones going at once. That's normal, and seeing it written down makes it easier to decide which of the long ones to finish next and which to let go.
