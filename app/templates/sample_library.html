Why a Sample Library Helps

This is a fictional example, not a copy of a user account and not a live feed of private data. It shows how someone might organize movies, TV shows, anime, games, albums, and books. Use it to decide which details would help you remember your own media, then try the controls in the interactive demo.

The fictional library below favors practical decisions over perfect cataloging. A useful tracker should answer ordinary questions: what am I watching now, what did I finish, what do I want to revisit, which notes are private, and which reviews are polished enough to share? Those details are what separate a living media library from a thin list of titles.

Example Records

Movie

Signal Harbor

Status: Watched. Rating: 8.5/10. Review choice: Public.

The public review focuses on pacing, character payoff, and whether the ending feels earned. The writer leaves personal details and plot spoilers out of this shared review.

TV Show

Northline Dispatch

Watched: Unchecked. Last watched: Season 2, episode 4. Review choice: Private.

Update progress records the last episode finished. The separate Private reminder says “Start episode 5 next time; watch with Sam.” Neither changes the rating or the show's total episode count.

Anime

Glass Circuit

Watched: Unchecked. Last watched: Episode 3, with no season specified. Review choice: Not reviewed yet.

The viewer uses continuous episode numbering, so the season stays blank. A short checkpoint reminder makes this easy to resume without writing a review before forming an opinion.

Game

Lantern Vale

Played: Unchecked. Rating: Not rated yet. Review choice: Private notes now, public review later.

The note tracks combat feel, accessibility settings, and whether the side quests still feel fresh after ten hours. Completion status can be updated when the credits roll.

Music

Late Map Atlas

Status: Listened. Format: Album. Review choice: Public short review.

The review mentions favorite tracks, the mood it fits, and whether the album works best as a full listen or as individual songs in a playlist.

Book

The Orchard Index

Read: Unchecked. Last read: Chapter 9. Review choice: Private.

The reader chose chapters because they move between editions with different page numbers. The checkpoint reminder says “Start the orchard letters next.” A page checkpoint would work too; the book keeps one position at a time.

A Weekly Cleanup Workflow

Once a week, this reader checks unfinished items and decides what still sounds appealing. They update a show's last watched episode, change a book's checkpoint to the last chapter read, and mark a finished album Listened. “Paused,” “planned,” and “playing” describe their intentions; they are not extra completion states in the app. A short private review or checkpoint reminder can hold that context.

Clear progress is useful when starting a show or book again: it removes only the checkpoint and its reminder. The title, completion choice, rating, and review stay in place. Before a larger cleanup, the reader exports a JSON backup, which includes private checkpoints and reminders, and stores it somewhere private.

The cleanup also checks review quality. Short private notes are fine for memory, but public reviews should contain enough context to help someone else. Instead of posting "good" or "boring," the user expands public notes with what worked, what audience might enjoy it, and whether the rating reflects craft, personal mood, or rewatch value. That makes the public review feed more useful for visitors and keeps the private library honest for the account owner.

Privacy Choices in the Sample

In this sample, movie and album reviews are published because they are polished and spoiler-safe. The TV, game, and book reviews are not published to public review pages; keeping their categories private also prevents friends from reading those notes. Episode, page, and chapter checkpoints and their reminders always stay private; publishing a review does not publish a checkpoint.

Friend visibility can be adjusted by category separately from public review choices. Letting friends see TV shows does not reveal episode checkpoints or their reminders. Copying a public collection or saving a reviewed title also does not copy the author's personal progress.

What Statistics Would Show

With these six records, completion statistics count one watched movie and one listened album: two finished titles and four unfinished. A checkpoint at episode 4 or chapter 9 does not increase the completion count. Ratings show reactions, while review coverage shows which titles have a written note or review. A checkpoint reminder stays separate from review coverage.

This is why OmniTrackr keeps media types together. A spreadsheet can count entries, but a focused app can make the routine easier: add the item, update the status, write a note, decide whether it is public, and let the dashboard summarize the pattern over time.
