Getting Started

Create a free account at OmniTrackr by signing up with your email and a password. After you verify your email, sign in to see the main dashboard with tabs for Movies, TV Shows, Anime, Video Games, Music, and Books. Use the tab bar to switch between categories. Your data is saved to your account and will be there whenever you sign in from any device.

Tracking Media

  1. Choose Movies, TV Shows, Anime, Video Games, Music, or Books. Open the category's Add form and enter the title. Use title search where available, then check the returned details before saving: editions and similarly named titles can differ.
  2. Leave the rating blank if you have not formed an opinion. The completion checkbox is separate: Watched, Played, Listened, or Read records completion, not whether you liked the item.
  3. For an unfinished item, leave completion unchecked. TV shows, anime, and books have a separate Update progress action for your stopping point. The completion checkbox does not have separate paused and dropped options.
  4. Save, then use Edit on the library entry to change its details. On phones and tablets, More details expands the card; View review opens the longer note or review.

Example: returning to a show after a break

Choose Update progress on the saved show, enter episode 4 and season 2, and save. This means episode 4 is the last one watched. Add a Private reminder such as “Watch episode 5 with Sam.” Keep Watched unchecked until the show is finished; the progress control does not change it, your rating, your review, or the show's episode count.

Books, specials, and clearing a checkpoint

For a book, choose page or chapter and enter the last one read. A book keeps one position, so changing the unit replaces its previous page or chapter when saved. TV and anime seasons are optional; season 0 is available for specials. Clear progress removes only the checkpoint and its reminder. These details stay private even if you share the category with friends or publish a review. You can practice in the demo.

Why tracking helps

Keeping a list of what you've watched or played helps you avoid forgetting titles, spot gaps in a series, and see how your tastes change over time. It also makes it easier to recommend things to friends and to get recommendations based on your real history.

Find the Right Part of Your Library

Use the completion buttons below a category's search and sort controls to show all titles, unfinished titles, or completed titles. Labels fit the category: for example, Books has Not read and Read. Add Unrated to find blank ratings; a score of zero still counts as rated. These filters cover the whole library, including titles on later pages, and combine with search and sorting.

Example: books you finished but haven't rated

Open Books, choose Read, then check Unrated. Edit a matching book when you are ready to rate it; after saving a score, it leaves this view because it is now rated. You can try this with a fictional book in the interactive demo.

TV shows, anime, and books also offer Has saved progress. This finds a saved episode, page, or chapter checkpoint; it does not infer whether you are actively watching or reading. Each category remembers its filters while this page stays open. Opening a specific title from global search or Next Up clears the destination's filters so that title can be shown. Clear search & filters restores all titles while keeping your sort order.

Ratings and Reviews

OmniTrackr ratings use a 0–10 scale. A blank rating means unrated; zero is an actual score. Choose a convention you can repeat: for example, 5 for mixed feelings, 7 for something you enjoyed, and 9 for a personal favorite. These are suggested anchors, not rules about what a work deserves. If you previously used five stars, doubling a score is a simple conversion, but leave uncertain scores blank instead of inventing precision.

The Public Review checkbox belongs to each item's Add or Edit form. Leave it unchecked for a private note. Checking it opts that review into public sharing, subject to the site's review-quality and moderation rules; it is not a promise of immediate publication. Category privacy and tab visibility are separate controls, not substitutes for checking the review's audience.

For example, “Watch with Sam next Friday” belongs in a private note. A public review should instead explain a useful judgment: “The slower first half gives the final decision weight; a good fit if you enjoy character conversations more than action.” Avoid personal details and mark spoilers. The review guide has fuller before-and-after examples.

Statistics

The Statistics tab shows overview numbers and charts: how many items you've added per category, average ratings, and trends. Use it to see which categories you use most, how your ratings distribute, and how your activity has grown. The dashboard updates as you add or edit entries.

Social Features and Friends

You can send and accept friend requests by username. Before sharing, distinguish three decisions: which tabs you want to see in your own dashboard, which categories friends may view, and which individual reviews you want public. Hiding a tab is a layout preference, not a privacy setting. Review the category privacy controls in account settings and each item's Public Review checkbox separately. For a curated public collection, check that collection's own publishing controls as well.

Import and Export

Choose the workflow that matches your file. A JSON export is a backup; a CSV from another tracker is a migration source. In account settings, Import Studio previews supported CSV files before you apply an import. Inspect converted ratings, completion, and private notes, and use the row filter and page controls to review every outcome. Fix unexpected results before confirming all ready rows; filtering the preview does not select rows for import. Opening the importer or previewing a file does not save titles.

Keep the original source file and export a backup before making a large change. Progress checkpoints and their private reminders are included in JSON backups, so protect those files. Try a small sample first and compare titles, completion, and ratings afterward. Matching records are skipped by the CSV importer rather than overwritten, so use Edit when you intend to correct an existing entry. The export and import guide explains the separate backup and migration workflows.

Tips

  • Add items soon after you finish them so you don't forget.
  • Use consistent rating scales to make statistics more meaningful.
  • Choose Public Review only on entries you intentionally want to share.
  • Export periodically as a backup.
  • Adjust privacy per category so friends see only what you're comfortable sharing.
