OmniTrackr Changelog

What changed, why it changed, and how each update improves the media tracking experience.

Why Publish a Changelog?

See what has changed in your library and which new controls are available. Each update explains what you can do and how it affects your existing tracking.

October 28, 2026: Monthly memberships on Ko-fi

Supporting OmniTrackr every month now comes with a few extras on top of the usual supporter perks. Nothing changes for free members, and one-time supporters keep everything they had.

  • Popcorn ($3 a month) adds a member tag beside your badge and three new profile banners: neon, marquee and comic.
  • Season Pass ($5) lets your banner move, with a slow sheen and chasing marquee bulbs. It stays still for anyone who has reduced motion turned on.
  • Director's Cut ($10) unlocks gold from the first month and puts your name in the credits at the top of the supporter wall, if you're on it.
  • Cancel any time on Ko-fi. Your extras last until the month you paid for ends, and your picks are saved if you come back. See the tiers.

October 29, 2026: Time spent and a binge calculator

The Statistics tab now estimates how many hours your library adds up to.

  • Finished titles count in full. Shows, anime, books, manga and podcasts you're partway into count up to the episode, page or chapter you last saved.
  • Lengths come from title pages where we have them (running times, episode lengths, page counts and RAWG's average playtime). Otherwise a typical length is used, and the section says how many titles that applies to.
  • You'll see the total, a breakdown by shelf and how much of it was finished this year. Download it as an image if you want to share it. The image is made on your device.
  • If your public profile shows statistics, it now shows estimated hours too, counted only from shelves you haven't made private.
  • The new binge calculator works out how long a show, anime or book series takes and the date you'd finish at your own pace. No account needed.

October 29, 2026: Remember why you added a title

Every title can now keep a short private note about where it came from, so a year later you still know who talked you into it.

  • Open a title's More menu and choose Why I added it. Pick a friend, type the name of someone else, or say it came from an article, a video or a podcast, and add a note if you like.
  • Titles you add from a friend's recommendation, the friends feed or a shared list are filled in for you. Anything you write yourself is never replaced.
  • The line under each title shows who recommended it. Click the name, or use the new Recommended by filter, to see everything on that shelf from the same person. Saved views remember it too.
  • The friends feed has a Your best recommenders card that ranks people by how you rated the picks you finished.
  • These notes are private. Friends, including the friend you credit, never see them. They are included in the JSON backup and in the spreadsheet export as why-added.csv, and a restore only fills in titles that don't have a note yet.

October 29, 2026: Security check of this week's features

We went back over the exports, tags, saved views and episode calendar added this week.

  • The spreadsheet export now includes your tags in a tags.csv file, so they leave with the rest of your library. The privacy policy now lists tags and saved views.
  • Custom tab column names that start with = + - or @ get an apostrophe in the export, like every other cell, so a spreadsheet app shows them as text.
  • Calendar feeds leave out any link from an episode source that contains a line break or a space, so a bad link can't add anything else to your calendar.
  • Episode schedules are checked against the cache in batches, and only a few new calendars are filled at once, so busy hours stay inside TVmaze's limits.

October 28, 2026: Tags and saved views for your library

You can now label titles your own way and come back to a filtered shelf with one click.

  • Open a title's More menu and choose Add tags. Use whatever labels help you, like "comfort watch", "with Sam" or "on Netflix". A title can have up to ten.
  • Tags show under each title. Click one, or pick it from the new Tag filter, to see every title on that shelf with the same tag. It works with search, sort and the other filters.
  • Once you've set up a search, sort or filter you like, choose Save this view and give it a name. Saved views sit above the filters, and each shelf keeps up to twelve.
  • Tags and saved views are private. Friends, public profiles and shared lists never see them.

October 28, 2026: This week's episodes in the weekly email

The weekly email now tells you which of your shows have a new episode in the next seven days, and what's first in your Next Up queue.

  • Every TV show and anime you haven't marked as watched counts, one line per show. A week with new episodes gets an email even when nothing else is coming out.
  • Next Up gets one line with the title at the top of your queue. It never sends an email on its own.
  • Account Settings has a new Emails section with a switch for the weekly email and one for occasional news. The weekly email stays off until you turn it on, and if you already get it, nothing changes.

October 28, 2026: New episodes in your calendar

The private release calendar can now list every new episode of the shows you're in the middle of, next to the premieres and new seasons it already had.

  • Open "Add to my calendar" on the "Coming up for you" card and tick the episodes box. Each TV show and anime you haven't marked as watched gets an entry for every episode airing in the next two months, starting with the ones you've saved progress on.
  • Episodes with a known air time show up at that time in your own time zone. Streaming drops without a set time appear as all-day entries. TV dates come from TVmaze and anime dates from AniList.
  • Calendars turned on before today keep exactly what they had until you tick the box. New calendars start with episodes on, and you can untick it whenever you like.
  • Your calendar address, ratings, notes and the rest of your library stay as they were.

October 28, 2026: Export your library for spreadsheets, Letterboxd and Goodreads

Account Settings has a new export panel next to the Import Studio. The JSON backup is still there, and three new downloads sit beside it.

  • Spreadsheets: a ZIP of CSV files with your whole library in one sheet, plus your journal, collections, progress and custom tabs.
  • Letterboxd: watched films with their diary dates and rewatches, and a separate watchlist file.
  • Goodreads and The StoryGraph: your books in the Goodreads layout, on "read" or "to-read".
  • Exporting only reads your library. The export guide explains how ratings are converted for each site.

October 28, 2026: A shelf for podcasts

Podcasts now have their own library tab, the eighth shelf after films, TV, anime, games, music, books and manga.

  • Search by name to fill in the host, genres, episode count and cover art from the Apple Podcasts directory, or type them in yourself. Save the episode you reached with Update progress, the same way you do for a TV show.
  • Tick Listened when you finish a limited series or catch up with an ongoing show. Podcasts count in Statistics, your Year in Review, badges, goals, Next Up, collections, public reviews and title pages, and you can hide the tab or make the shelf private in Settings.
  • Nothing already in your library changes, and older backups import as before. The podcast tracker guide has tips for long back catalogues and shows that never end.

October 27, 2026: A shelf for manga

Manga, manhwa, manhua and webtoons now have their own library tab next to Books.

  • Search by title to fill in the mangaka, first published year, genres, volumes and chapters from MyAnimeList, or type them in yourself. Save the chapter you stopped on the same way you save a page in a book.
  • Manga counts everywhere your other shelves do: Statistics (with volume and chapter totals), your Year in Review, badges, goals, Next Up, collections, public reviews and title pages. You can hide or make the shelf private in Settings like any other category.
  • AniList and MyAnimeList imports now put manga on the Manga shelf instead of Books. Titles an earlier import already put on your Books shelf are left where they are and shown as already saved, so nothing is duplicated. Read the manga tracker guide for tips.

October 27, 2026: Episode-by-episode tracking

Keeping your place in a TV show or anime now takes one tap.

  • TV and anime rows in your library show a progress bar and where you are, like Watched S2 E4 · 13 of 19. Press + S2 E5 after you watch the next episode. Pressed it by mistake? Undo puts it back.
  • Open Episodes for a season-by-season grid. Tap an episode to mark it and everything before it watched. The episode list comes from TVmaze for TV and MyAnimeList for anime, or from the episode count you saved with the title.
  • The same bar and next-episode button appear on your Continue card and in Next Up. Your place stays private, keeps your saved reminder, and never changes a title's rating, review or finished status.

October 27, 2026: Steadier dashboard, easier reading

A tidy-up of how pages load and read. Nothing in your library changes.

  • Your dashboard no longer jumps around while its cards load. The cards, your library and the footer appear together, in place.
  • Goals, the monthly challenge, the seasonal card and badges are fetched once when the dashboard opens instead of twice.
  • Shared list pages keep their heading still while the list loads, and screen readers no longer read the whole list again after every vote.
  • Notification times, diary days still to come, the account's verified line and a few homepage and demo labels are easier to read in light and dark mode.

October 25, 2026: Friends feed

See what your friends have been watching, playing, listening to and reading, all in one place.

  • Open Friends feed from the dashboard tabs, or See what friends are up to in the Friends panel. It lists what friends added, finished, rated and reviewed over the last 30 days, newest first. A green dot on the tab means something new arrived since you last looked.
  • Filter by Finished, Ratings & reviews or Added. A big import shows as a single card so it doesn't bury everything else.
  • Press Cheer to give a friend a high five. They get a notification. Like the look of something? Add to Next Up or Add to library copies the title and its public details, never your friend's rating or review.
  • The feed only shows what friends could already see in your library: categories you made private stay out, and reviews you marked as spoilers stay folded. Don't want to show up at all? Untick Share my updates with friends at the top of the feed. If you turn it back on later, only new updates are shown.

October 24, 2026: Recommend a title to a friend

Found something a friend has to try? Send it to them with a short note about why they'll love it.

  • Pick Recommend from a title's More menu in your library, or press Recommend to a friend on any title page. Choose one or more friends and add a note if you like.
  • Your friend sees it under Recommended for you on their dashboard and gets a notification. They can add it to their library or their Next Up in one tap, or pass on it. You hear back when they take your tip. If they pass, you're not told.
  • Friend not on OmniTrackr yet? Share a link instead. The page shows your pick and your note. When they join from it, you become friends and the title is waiting on their dashboard.
  • Only the title and its public details are sent, never your rating, review or notes. Nothing is added to anyone's library until they choose to add it.

October 22, 2026: A handier Next Up queue

Next Up, the list on your dashboard of what you plan to watch, read or play next, now does more of the work for you.

  • Press Done on a queued title to mark it finished and take it off the queue in one step. The usual short reflection opens, and your rating and review stay as they were.
  • Each title shows its cover, and titles you finish somewhere else are marked so you can clear them all at once.
  • When you manage the queue, a new button moves a title straight to the top.
  • Titles already in your queue now say "In Next Up" in your library, and Pick for Me can put a suggestion in your queue right after adding it.
  • Your queue and its order are kept exactly as they were.

October 20, 2026: Pin titles to your profile

Your public profile can now open with a showcase: titles you pick from your library, in the order you choose. Set it up under Account → Public profile.

  • Every member can pin three titles. Supporters can pin six.
  • Supporters can also put a banner across the top of their profile: aurora, film strip, pixels, pages, vinyl or stars, drawn in their accent color.
  • Titles from a category you keep private can't be pinned, and an old pin disappears if you later make its category private or delete the title.
  • Nothing changes on your profile until you pin something or pick a banner.

October 20, 2026: Shared lists

You can now build a list with other people: a watchlist for the two of you, an anime queue for the group chat, or a shortlist for book club.

  • Add friends to a list in one click, or send its link. Someone without an account can see what's on the list, sign up, and land back on it.
  • Everyone can add movies, shows, anime, games, books and albums, and each title shows who added it. Tap "Count me in" on the ones you want, and the titles most people want move to the top.
  • Mark a title watched, played or read when you get to it, let "Pick one for us" choose for you, or copy any title into your own library. A list never reads or changes anyone's library.
  • Find your lists under Shared lists in your library. You'll get a notification when someone joins or adds something, and only one until you open the list again.

October 19, 2026: Play through your Year in Review

Your Year in Review now plays as a set of full-screen slides you tap through, and ends with an image sized for Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp stories.

  • New on the recap: your longest streak of days with something logged, your rating style, the director, artist or author you finished most, the badges you earned that year, and a one-line type for your year.
  • Shared recap links play the same slides. The story image, like the rest of a shared recap, leaves out shelves you keep private and never includes notes or reviews.
  • In December and January the weekly email links to your recap when you finished something that year, and your public profile links to it once you share it.

October 19, 2026: Member charts

Every kind of title now has a chart of what OmniTrackr members rate highest, built from the ratings in their own libraries.

  • Charts for movies, TV shows, anime, games, albums and books rank titles by a weighted member average, with a short quote from a public review where there is one.
  • A title needs at least three member ratings to chart. Members who keep a category private are never counted, and the charts never show who rated what.
  • Title pages show where a title ranks and, once five members have rated it, how those ratings split from "below 5" to "9 to 10".

October 18, 2026: Easier to use with a keyboard, a screen reader or a phone

We checked the signed-in app page by page with an automated accessibility checker, a keyboard and a phone-sized screen, and fixed what we found.

  • Account settings, friend requests, custom tabs and the other pop-up windows now announce themselves to screen readers, take keyboard focus when they open, keep the Tab key inside, and return you to where you were when they close.
  • Editing a title in your library moves the cursor straight to its first field, and Save or Cancel brings you back to the same row.
  • A "Skip to your library" link is the first thing the Tab key reaches.
  • Violet buttons, teal labels and some faint text in light mode are darker, so they are easier to read.
  • On touch screens, footer links and small buttons are at least 44 pixels tall, and the account settings fields are full width with the right hints for password managers.

Nothing in your library, your settings or your public reviews changed.

October 17, 2026: Easier to find the guides

The free guides are now the first thing the site points you to.

  • The menu at the top of every public page leads with Discover, In order, If you liked and the Daily puzzle. Public reviews and collections are still linked at the bottom of every page.
  • The homepage shows the newest guides, with the date each was last checked.
  • The About page now says who runs OmniTrackr, how the guides are written and how the site is paid for, and the new editorial standards explain how facts are checked, how spoilers are handled and how to send a correction.
  • The site map lists every guide, and the Daily archive shows the first clue of each past puzzle. Finished puzzles link to the guide about their answer when there is one.

Nothing in your library, your settings or your public reviews changed.

October 15, 2026: OmniTrackr Daily

OmniTrackr Daily is a new free guessing game: one movie, TV show, anime, game, book or album a day, guessed from six clues.

  • Every wrong guess or skip reveals a clue that is a little easier than the last. You have six tries.
  • Share your result as a row of squares that does not give the answer away, and keep a daily streak going.
  • When the game ends, read the editors' note on the title and add it to your library, or save it to your list before signing up.
  • Every past puzzle stays playable in the archive, with its answer and clues behind a spoiler.
  • Your games and streak are kept only in your browser. Nothing about how you play is sent to your account.

October 15, 2026: Tier List Maker

Rank anything from S to D with the new tier list maker, then save it as an image to share.

  • Start from a ready-made set, like this season's anime, the titles members track most, or the editors' picks, or search for titles and add any title by name.
  • Drag titles into tiers with a mouse, or tap a title and choose its tier on a phone. Rename any tier.
  • Members can turn their own ratings into a tier list in one click. Your library is not changed.
  • Boards are saved only in your browser. No account is needed, and nothing about your list is sent to OmniTrackr.

October 13, 2026: Release Dates in Your Calendar

Release Radar dates can now live in the calendar you already use, and they update on their own.

  • Every Release Radar page has Google Calendar, Apple Calendar and Outlook buttons. Subscribe to the highlights, or to just movies, TV premieres, the anime season or games. No account needed.
  • Members can turn on a private calendar from the "Coming up for you" card. It lists only new seasons, sequels and release days connected to titles in their library.
  • The private calendar shows titles and dates only. Ratings, notes and anything else in your library stay private. You can get a new address or turn it off at any time, and the old address stops working straight away.
  • Nothing in your existing library changes.

October 12, 2026: Pick for Me

Can't decide what to start? The new Pick for me page suggests one title at a time.

  • Choose a format, or leave it on Anything, and get one movie, TV show, anime, game, book or album with the reason it was chosen and a caveat to read first.
  • Every pick comes from OmniTrackr's own Discover trails, monthly editions and editors' collections. Nothing is based on anyone's private library.
  • Members can add a pick to their library in one tap. It is added as not started, and a title you already have is never changed.
  • Visitors can save picks to a list without an account. The list moves into the new library at first log-in.
  • Today's pick on the dashboard now has a "Find something new" link for when your own list runs dry.

October 12, 2026: Search Before You Sign Up

The homepage now opens with a search box, so you can start your list before making an account.

  • Type a movie, show, anime, game, album or book you love and tap a result to put it on your list.
  • The search covers titles OmniTrackr members already track. It never shows who tracks them, and categories members keep private are left out.
  • Your list stays in your browser until you create a free account. It moves into your new library the first time you log in.

October 12, 2026: Your Challenge in the Weekly Email

The weekly email now includes this month's challenge.

  • If you've taken the challenge, the email shows how many steps you've done, how many days are left, and the next step.
  • If you haven't, there's a short line about this month's challenge with a link to take it. This line stops appearing in the last week of the month.
  • The challenge never triggers an email by itself. Quiet weeks stay quiet.
  • Year in Review now ends with a short note about how OmniTrackr is paid for. Supporters see a thank-you instead.

October 11, 2026: Bring Your IMDb and StoryGraph History

The Import Studio now reads IMDb and The StoryGraph exports, alongside Letterboxd, Goodreads, and MyAnimeList.

  • An IMDb ratings, watchlist, or list file fills Movies, TV Shows, and Games in one go. Rated titles are marked watched, and episode ratings are listed rather than imported.
  • A StoryGraph file brings your books, with quarter-star ratings kept: 4.25 stars becomes 8.5/10.
  • The preview now says which format it recognized. As before, nothing is saved until you confirm, and titles you already track are never overwritten.
  • A new switching guide shows where each service keeps its export and what comes across.

October 10, 2026: Monthly Challenges

Each month now has a small, optional challenge with three steps, starting with October's "Stay with the strange".

  • Choose Take the challenge on your dashboard. Progress fills in from what you mark finished that month, including anything you finished before joining.
  • When every step is done you get a notification and can share a card that shows the challenge and its steps, never your titles.
  • There are no streaks or rankings. You can skip a month or leave a challenge at any time, and your library is never changed. Each challenge has a written guide at omnitrackr.xyz/challenges.

October 10, 2026: Notifications on Your Phone and Desktop

Your OmniTrackr notifications can now reach you even when the site is closed.

  • Open the notification bell and choose Turn on to get friend requests, takes, goals reached and release day alerts on that device.
  • It works in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, and in the installed app. On iPhone and iPad, add OmniTrackr to your Home Screen first.
  • It is off until you turn it on, it can be turned off from the same place, and signing out stops notifications on that device. Messages are encrypted for your device.

October 10, 2026: Know the Day It Comes Out

OmniTrackr now tells you when something connected to your library is released.

  • On release day you get a notification for titles you track on Release Radar, and for new seasons and sequels of shows, anime, films, and games already in your library.
  • Several releases on the same day arrive as one notification, and each release is announced only once, even if you dismiss it.
  • Alerts appear in the notification bell inside OmniTrackr. Nothing is emailed or shared, and your library is not changed.

September 28, 2026: One Look Across the Public Site

Every public page now shares the homepage's design, header, and footer.

  • Guides and tracking pages use a dark reading layout with clearer steps, callouts, and tables.
  • Public reviews put search and media-type shortcuts up front; each review page has a larger header and suggests more reviews in the same category.
  • Titles without artwork show a placeholder instead of a profile icon.
  • On phones, every section is one tap away in the menu.
  • About, FAQ, privacy and policy pages, the roadmap, site map, demo, and community collections now use the same design.
  • FAQ answers are always visible, including without JavaScript.
  • Broken or old links now land on a helpful page with ways back instead of a plain error.

September 27, 2026: Release Radar

The new Release Radar shows what is coming out across movies, TV, anime, and video games, and lets members track a title in one click.

  • Browse this month's films, TV premieres and new seasons, the full anime season, and games with a firm date, plus a few months ahead.
  • Read Radar notes calculated from each list: the busiest weeks, returning versus new shows, platform spread, and more.
  • Track a title to add it to your library as unfinished and to a private Release Radar collection. Existing entries are reused, never duplicated or overwritten.
  • See how many OmniTrackr libraries already hold a title. Only a count is shown, never who saved it.
  • A redesigned homepage puts the vortex front and center, shows what is coming out soon, and makes signing up or logging in quicker on phones.
  • The landing animation now loads as a lightweight WebP (about 96% smaller than before) and switches to a still frame when your device asks for reduced motion.

September 24, 2026: Find the Guide for Your Next Step

Guides now starts with four practical choices: start a library, bring an existing list, resume a title, and choose what to share.

  • Read guides in a consistent layout with clearer navigation, section links, and room for tables on small screens.
  • Compare tracking routines using official sources, honest limitations, and steps for previewing an import.
  • Move from an example to the demo, Import Studio instructions, or Discover when you are ready.
  • See how category sharing with friends differs from publishing a review, and where to keep reminders visible only to you.

September 24, 2026: Find What You Haven't Finished or Rated

Quick filters help you browse a large library without searching one title at a time.

  • Show unfinished or completed titles in any of the six media categories, and combine either view with Unrated.
  • Find saved episode, page, and chapter checkpoints with Has saved progress for TV, anime, and books.
  • Combine filters with search, sorting, and pagination. Clear search and filters in one click.
  • Try Books, Read, and Unrated in the public demo to find a fictional finished book ready for a rating.

Filters change what you see; your saved records and sharing choices stay intact.

September 24, 2026: A Calmer Place for Your Library

OmniTrackr has a more consistent appearance, with violet and teal accents, quieter backgrounds, and clearer controls in light and dark mode.

  • Find search and Add Anything in a cleaner header, with media libraries and personal tools grouped into separate navigation rows.
  • Use consistent search, sort, and action controls across all six media categories, with clearer desktop tables and mobile cards.
  • Read labeled add forms and use dialogs that match your chosen theme, including account settings, Import Studio, and progress.
  • Open Friends from a labeled button without shifting your library. The panel fits your friend list and scrolls when it reaches the available screen height.

Your tracking features, keyboard shortcuts, saved records, and sharing choices stay available.

September 2026: Know What Your Import Will Save

Import Studio now shows converted ratings, completion, and private notes before you confirm a CSV import.

  • Review every row with pages and filters for ready, duplicate, and invalid entries.
  • Corrected music completion and explicit column mappings. Invalid values identify the field to fix in your source file.
  • Changing files or options cancels outdated previews. Failed requests keep your file available for another preview.
  • Keep a completion summary and open the affected library categories directly.

Imports remain additive: existing records keep their own values and imported notes stay private. The import guide includes worked examples and format limits.

September 2026: Find the Right Starting Point

Three Release RadarDiscover trails now offer deeper guides to choosing your next watch, read, listen, or play.

  • One evening, well spent compares attention, emotional tone, and satisfying stopping points.
  • Finding your feet explores different kinds of fresh starts, from everyday independence to growing responsibility.
  • Beautifully strange worlds helps you choose how much uncertainty and exploration you want, without giving away discoveries.

Each guide includes a comparison, individual notes, related reading, and verified sources with an explanation of our editorial method. Read freely, then preview and save the picks you want to your private library.

September 2026: Less Scrolling, More Tracking

Search, Add Anything, and category navigation now sit above your daily suggestions, with account controls in their own row on smaller screens.

  • Continue puts two recent unfinished titles within reach. Expand Make it memorable when you want to rate or reflect on a finished title.
  • Next Up starts with a three-title preview. Use Manage queue to see everything and change the order; your saved order stays intact.
  • Open a suggestion or queued title directly, even when matching titles span several library pages.

Your records, private checkpoints, and sharing choices are unchanged.

September 2026: Pick Up Where You Left Off

Keep a private checkpoint for a show or book without editing its review or marking the whole title finished.

  • Use Update progress on TV shows and anime to save the last watched episode, with an optional season. Season 0 supports specials.
  • For books, choose the last read page or chapter. Add a short Private reminder for next time.
  • Keep completion, ratings, reviews, and title details separate. Clear progress removes only the checkpoint and reminder.
  • Checkpoints remain private even when friends can see a category or you publish a review. They are included in JSON backups.
  • Try the same workflow with fictional titles in the interactive demo. Sample edits reset on reload and never enter your account.

September 2026: Make a Shared Collection Your Own

Found a collection you want to keep? Preview its titles, choose your favorites, and save a private copy.

  • Return to your chosen collection after signing in or creating an account in the same tab.
  • See which titles are already in your library. Existing ratings, reviews, and progress stay yours.
  • Retry an interrupted save without creating another copy, then open your saved collection directly.

Saving requires your confirmation. New titles start unfinished and unrated, and later changes to the original collection do not alter your copy.

September 2026: Try Tracking Before Signup

The interactive demo lets you practice with a sample library before creating an account.

  • Browse media categories, add a sample title, mark it finished, and try a rating or note.
  • See library statistics update from your changes, then reset the samples whenever you like.
  • When you are ready, create your own library and use Add Anything to find your first real title.

Practice entries stay in the demo page and reset when it reloads. They are never published or copied into your account. The demo has no ads.

September 2026: From Community Reviews to Your Library

Public Reviews now puts member opinions first, with title search, media filters, and a clearer mobile layout.

  • Reviews stay visible while new results load. A failed request can be retried without losing the current list.
  • Save a reviewed title after previewing your library, with a return to the same preview after signing in.
  • Existing titles keep your own notes, ratings and progress. New titles start unfinished and unrated.
  • Open the exact saved title in your library when you are ready to continue.

Publishing a review remains a separate choice. Saving a recommendation does not publish your own notes.

September 2026: Faster Results in Add Anything

Search results now appear as each media source responds. You can choose a title while other sources are still searching.

  • A slow or unavailable source keeps its own retry button without clearing successful results.
  • Changing your search, choosing another media type, or closing the dialog cancels obsolete lookups.
  • Manual entry stays above the results, ready to use while a search runs.

Choosing a result fills the existing entry form for you to review. Your library changes only when you save that form.

September 2026: From Discovery to Your Library

Discover now helps you find a starting point and keep it through signup or sign-in.

  • Search trails by title, creator, or theme, and narrow them by media format. Each card previews the titles inside.
  • Return to the same recommendation after signing in, review your library matches, and save only the picks you choose.
  • Open your saved collection directly from the confirmation.
  • Fixed email-verification and password-reset links opening the ordinary login form for signed-out visitors.

Recommendations remain readable without an account. Existing ratings, notes, progress, and sharing choices are preserved when you save.

September 2026: A Calmer Way Back In

The Welcome Back Deck turns existing private library signals into one focused return moment instead of adding another permanent dashboard widget.

  • Offers the deck only after a successful login following at least three full days away and only after five built-in titles have been saved.
