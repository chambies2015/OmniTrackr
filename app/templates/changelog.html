November 13, 2026: A new look for the friends feed, watch clubs and discussions

The three places where you talk to other people on OmniTrackr now match the rest of the app.

  • The friends feed has a Friends / Following switch at the top, so updates from people you follow are one tap away instead of at the bottom of a long page. Updates are grouped under Today, Yesterday, This week and Earlier.
  • Your sharing switch, your best recommenders and people you might follow sit in a column beside the feed on a wide screen.
  • A review in the feed that hides a twist in ||double bars|| keeps it covered until you tap it, the same as in discussions.
  • On a watch club, a bar shows who has finished and who is partway through, and the club score gets its own badge. Club talk reads like a chat, with your own posts tinted.
  • List cards on the shared lists page show their first four covers on a shelf, and the current club pick with its poster and finish-by date.
  • Discussion boards and threads show each member's initial in a coloured circle, and the reply count sits in a speech bubble beside each thread.

Nothing about your lists, clubs, posts or feed settings changes.

November 12, 2026: A new Statistics page, and Settings one section at a time

The Statistics tab used to be ten collapsed rows, one per format plus a library summary, and you had to open each to see anything. It now opens on your whole library: titles saved, how many you've finished, your average rating, how you spread your ratings from 1 to 10, and which decades your titles come from.

  • "Finished, by format" shows how far along each shelf is. Pick a row to see that format on its own below.
  • Formats are tabs now, so one click swaps between movies, TV shows, anime, games, music, books, manga, podcasts and audiobooks. Each tab keeps what the old rows had: ratings, release decades, directors, seasons and episodes, volumes and chapters, listening hours and genres.
  • Time spent, Monthly Replay and Tasteprint sit on the same page as before, and buttons at the top jump to each one.
  • The format tabs work from the keyboard with the arrow keys. The old rows could only be opened with a mouse or a tap.

Settings shows one section at a time. Choosing Privacy or Notifications in the menu swaps the page to that section. Before, the menu scrolled you through one long column that ran to about 18 phone screens. Links that open a particular setting, such as the ones in emails, still land in the right place.

  • Switches that are off are easier to tell from the page behind them in the light theme.
  • "See memberships" and "Support on Ko-fi" look like buttons again. They had lost their padding and looked like highlighted text.
  • The four supporter tiers fit on one row on a wide screen, and the showcase search results scroll inside their own box.

Nothing in your library or your saved settings changes. Both pages work in light and dark and in every supporter theme.

November 12, 2026: Awards season, with a ballot for each show

A new page at /awards keeps the dates for The Game Awards, the Golden Globes, the Grammys and the Oscars in one place, with a written page for each: when nominees come out, who votes, where to watch and last year's winners.

  • When a show announces its nominees, its page becomes a ballot. Pick one nominee per category, skip any you like, and change your mind until the show starts.
  • Signed in, picks save as you make them. Signed out, they stay in your browser and move to your account if you make one.
  • Nominees you already track are marked, and the others can be added to your library from the ballot. The same works for last year's winners.
  • After the show, every category you called is a point. Skipped categories don't count against you.
  • You can share a ballot as a link and a card. It shows your picks and nothing from your library, and "Stop sharing" ends the link.
  • No nominee lists are out yet, so today each page shows its dates and a "Tell me when the ballot opens" button. The Game Awards and Grammy nominees are due around November 16.
  • While a ballot is open, a small card on your home screen links to it, and it shows your score for two weeks after the show. "Hide" puts it away.

Nothing changes in your library unless you add a nominee yourself.

November 12, 2026: Group polls you can send, save and run again

A group poll lets the people you're watching with vote on a shortlist from one link. This update is about what happens around the vote: how the link looks when you send it, what you can do with the winner, and how to do it all again next week.

  • Poll links have their own preview picture. In a group chat the link shows the question and the titles on a ballot, and once voting closes it shows the winner.
  • The page you land on after making a poll, and the poll itself, have buttons for copying the link or sending it by WhatsApp, text message or email.
  • You can build a shortlist before you have an account. Search or tap popular titles, press "Get the link", and the shortlist is waiting for you after you sign up.
  • "Pick four for me" fills a shortlist from your Next Up queue and library, or from popular titles if you're not signed in. It sticks to one kind, so a film isn't put up against a whole series.
  • The result shows where the winner is streaming, with a button that puts it at the end of your Next Up queue. Voters without an account can save it to a list that moves into their library when they join.
  • After a poll closes, whoever made it can start another round with the titles that lost. The old link points to the new round, so the group can keep using the one already in their chat.
  • On a shared list, "Put it to a vote" starts a poll from the titles the list wants most.

Polls made before today work as they did. Adding a winner to Next Up never changes a title you already have: your rating, notes and progress stay as they are.

November 12, 2026: Security and speed check of the new sign-up forms and the binge calculator guides

We went over the two things that shipped on November 10: the new sign-up, log-in and password forms, and the 30 "how long does it take" pages in the binge calculator. We loaded the guides in a browser as a visitor and as a member, at desktop and phone sizes, opened plan links with made-up values, and went through signing up, asking for a reset link and opening one. No page had a script error or a broken link, and all 30 guides are long enough to be listed in search. These are the fixes.

  • Password reset and email confirmation links open the homepage with a one-time key in the address. The homepage also reports visits to Google Analytics, and Analytics records the address of the page it counts, so the key was sent along with it. A reset key works for an hour, or until it is used. The key is now taken out of the address Analytics is given.
  • Confirming an email was the one step of signing up with no limit on how often it could be called. It now has one, like signing up, logging in and resetting a password.
  • "What you have left" on the binge calculator compared every unfinished title in your library with every title in all 30 guides, twice. For a library with 1,600 unfinished titles that took about 290 milliseconds on the server. The guides are now indexed once and the same library takes about 40.
  • Each guide page worked out its links to title pages from the whole site search list on every view. They are now worked out once each time that list is rebuilt, which is every ten minutes.
  • The name of a plan can come from a shared link, and it goes into the file you get from "Add the finish date to my calendar". A name with one kind of line break in it could have added lines of its own to that file. It is now written out as text like the other kinds.
  • Fifteen of the guides had a page title too long for a search result, so the hours were the part that got cut off. Those now use a shorter question, such as "How long to read A Song of Ice and Fire? About 148 hours". The heading on the page is unchanged.

Your account, your password and your library are untouched.

November 10, 2026: How long 30 series take, and a planner that works back from a date

The binge calculator now has a page for each of 30 series, such as Breaking Bad, One Piece, the Harry Potter books and the Marvel films. Each one gives the total, then breaks it down season by season, arc by arc, book by book or film by film, and says what the total leaves out.

  • You can plan two ways: say how much time you have each day and get a finish date, or pick a date and see how much you need each day to make it.
  • Untick the seasons, books or films you've finished, or type how many episodes you've seen, and the plan counts only what is left.
  • Book pages switch between reading and listening, with your own reading pace or audiobook speed. Naruto can skip its filler, and The Lord of the Rings films can use the extended editions.
  • "Copy a link to this plan" gives you an address that opens the same plan for anyone, and "Add the finish date to my calendar" downloads a calendar entry.
  • The main calculator also takes films, audiobooks and games now, and can look up titles members track.
  • Signed in, each page reads your library: finished parts start unticked and a show starts from the episode you're on. The calculator also lists what you haven't finished and the hours each has left.
  • Without an account you can save a series to a list that moves into your library when you sign up.

Episode counts were checked against TVmaze, audiobook lengths against Audible and film running times against each film's Wikipedia page, all on November 10. Adding a series from one of these pages never changes anything you've already saved.

November 10, 2026: Signing up takes two boxes

The sign-up, log-in and password forms have a new look and fewer steps. They work the same way whether you use a password, Google, a passkey or a two-factor code.

  • Signing up asks for an email and a password. Your username is suggested from your email and shown in one line under the password, with a Change link if you want to pick your own. If the suggested name is taken, the form offers the next one for you to keep or replace.
  • If the email address looks like a slip, such as gmial.com, the form asks whether you meant gmail.com and fixes it in one click.
  • After you sign up, the form turns into a "Check your inbox" step. It names the address we wrote to, has a button that opens Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo Mail, iCloud Mail or Proton Mail when you use one of them, and can send the email again. If you open the link in another tab, the first tab notices and offers your library.
  • Asking for a password reset ends on the same step. Choosing a new password is one box with a Show button, and you land on the log-in form as soon as it is saved.
  • A confirmation link that has expired now takes you straight to a box where you can ask for a new one.
  • On a phone the form opens as a full page, so the whole sign-up form fits on one screen without scrolling.
  • Messages above the form are easier to spot: errors and confirmations each have their own color and mark.

Existing accounts, passwords, passkeys and two-factor settings are untouched.

November 9, 2026: Finding a cover no longer renames what you saved

When something in your library had no cover, OmniTrackr looked one up and took the first search result, even when it was a different record. That result could also replace the title you typed, and for albums and books the artist or author too, so a saved "Blonde" could turn into a single with another name.

  • Looking up a cover never changes your title, artist or author, in any of the libraries. It only fills in details you left blank, like a year, genre or release date.
  • For albums, books, anime and games, a cover is only added when the result's title (and the artist or author, where there is one) agrees with yours. Small differences such as accents, "The", "(Deluxe Edition)" or a subtitle still count, a full album is chosen over a single of the same name, and anime can match by its English or Japanese name.
  • If nothing matches well enough, the item stays without a cover instead of borrowing one from something else. Picking a title from search when you add it still fills everything in as before.

November 4, 2026: Share reviews you already wrote

A review starts private unless you switch on Public, and reviews imported from Letterboxd, Goodreads and the other sites always arrive private. So a lot of good writing sits where nobody can read it. If some of your private reviews are long enough to show on a title page, your home page now has a "Share your reviews" card listing them.

November 4, 2026: Security and accessibility check of gift lists and phone shares

We went over this week's additions: the gift guide and gift lists, sharing a title into OmniTrackr from another app, the winter anime guide, the lighter public pages and the search clean-up. We tried other people's gift items, forged undo keys, odd share links and long shared text, and ran an accessibility scanner over the new pages in light and dark mode. The scanner found nothing, nothing showed one member's data to another, and shares can't send you anywhere off the site. These are the fixes.
