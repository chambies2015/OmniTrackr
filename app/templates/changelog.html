OmniTrackr Changelog

What changed, why it changed, and how each update improves the media tracking experience.

Why Publish a Changelog?

See what has changed in your library and which new controls are available. Each update explains what you can do and how it affects your existing tracking.

The October Discover Monthly Edition follows uncertainty across six media types without turning the list into a Halloween checklist.

  • Compare a meditative film, limited horror series, episodic anime, conversational adventure game, double album, and classic novel by the kind of attention each asks for.
  • Read an original reason, practical starting point, candid content and pacing caveat, and first-party source for every pick.
  • Browse the September edition from the new monthly archive instead of replacing it.
  • Preview exact library matches and save any subset to a private collection without changing an existing rating, review, completion state, or progress record.
  • Publish stable article metadata and sitemap dates for both authored editions.

September 28, 2026: One Look Across the Public Site

Every public page now shares the homepage's design, header, and footer.

  • Guides and tracking pages use a dark reading layout with clearer steps, callouts, and tables.

September 27, 2026: Release Radar

The new Release Radar shows what is coming out across movies, TV, anime, and video games, and lets members track a title in one click.

  • Browse this month's films, TV premieres and new seasons, the full anime season, and games with a firm date, plus a few months ahead.
  • Read Radar notes calculated from each list: the busiest weeks, returning versus new shows, platform spread, and more.
  • Track a title to add it to your library as unfinished and to a private Release Radar collection. Existing entries are reused, never duplicated or overwritten.

  • A redesigned homepage puts the vortex front and center, shows what is coming out soon, and makes signing up or logging in quicker on phones.
  • The landing animation now loads as a lightweight WebP (about 96% smaller than before) and switches to a still frame when your device asks for reduced motion.

September 2026: Pick Up Where You Left Off

Keep a private checkpoint for a show or book without editing its review or marking the whole title finished.

  • Use Update progress on TV shows and anime to save the last watched episode, with an optional season. Season 0 supports specials.
  • For books, choose the last read page or chapter. Add a short Private reminder for next time.
  • Keep completion, ratings, reviews, and title details separate. Clear progress removes only the checkpoint and reminder.
  • Checkpoints remain private even when friends can see a category or you publish a review. They are included in JSON backups.
  • Try the same workflow with fictional titles in the interactive demo. Sample edits reset on reload and never enter your account.

Found a collection you want to keep? Preview its titles, choose your favorites, and save a private copy.

  • See which titles are already in your library. Existing ratings, reviews, and progress stay yours.

September 2026: From Community Reviews to Your Library

Public Reviews now puts member opinions first, with title search, media filters, and a clearer mobile layout.

Publishing a review remains a separate choice. Saving a recommendation does not publish your own notes.

September 2026: Faster Results in Add Anything

Search results now appear as each media source responds. You can choose a title while other sources are still searching.

  • A slow or unavailable source keeps its own retry button without clearing successful results.
  • Changing your search, choosing another media type, or closing the dialog cancels obsolete lookups.
  • Manual entry stays above the results, ready to use while a search runs.

Choosing a result fills the existing entry form for you to review. Your library changes only when you save that form.

September 2026: A Calmer Way Back In

The Welcome Back Deck turns existing private library signals into one focused return moment instead of adding another permanent dashboard widget.

  • Offers the deck only after a successful login following at least three full days away and only after five built-in titles have been saved.
  • Composes the existing Next Up queue, Today’s Pick, Library Pulse, and Activity Journal into a primary choice, an alternative, an optional reflection, and a compact recap.
  • Temporarily replaces Today’s Pick and Library Pulse while visible, then restores the familiar dashboard after an item is opened or the deck is dismissed.
  • Keeps eligibility and a signed random activation proof in short-lived browser session storage rather than creating a server-side visit history.
  • Adds only anonymous daily shown, opened, and dismissed totals to the existing site-health panel. A non-reversible digest of the random proof is retained for up to seven days to prevent duplicate counting; it cannot be joined to an account or media record.
  • Batches queue lookups, aggregates journal recaps in the database, and prevents the Welcome Back Deck from racing Today’s Pick and Library Pulse during dashboard startup.

The deck does not send email or push reminders, create streaks, publish activity, use AI recommendations, or load ads in the private dashboard.

September 2026: Automated Collection Discovery and Recovery

This release completes the existing public collection workflow without adding another feed, paid moderation service, or daily human approval queue.

  • Reused the collection quality floor, gallery, content hashes, signed visitor cookie, reports, owner notifications, and aggregate health view.
  • Added one deterministic readiness result for the collection editor, gallery, detail-page indexability, and sitemap.
  • Made qualifying published collections discoverable automatically while keeping private collections private and share-ready-only pages out of search.
  • Replaced free-text collection allegations with fixed reasons, one report per signed browser and version, and a two-per-hour IP rate limit.
  • Temporarily unlisted an exact collection version after three independent reports and notified its owner without deleting the collection.
  • Made an owner edit clear the current report state, rerun readiness, and restore a qualifying revision automatically.
  • Removed the manual action queue while retaining aggregate site health and a narrowly scoped emergency block.

Existing accounts, collections, library items, privacy choices, direct links, and historic public-content decisions are preserved. Collection pages remain outside the ad-loader allowlist while their quality and usefulness are measured.

September 2026: Automated Review Quality and Safety

This release makes public review discovery sustainable for a two-person project without introducing a manual moderation queue or paid AI service.

  • Reused the existing opt-in review feed and divided it into transparent community-ready and search-ready tiers.
  • Reserved standalone pages, structured review data, sitemap URLs, prominent ordering, and ad eligibility for search-ready reviews.
  • Added editor feedback for character count, word count, complete thoughts, varied language, and public safety checks.
  • Reused the signed public-visitor cookie for fixed-reason, rate-limited reports without storing reporter IP addresses, fingerprints, or free-text allegations.
  • Automatically unlisted an exact review version after three independent reports, notified the author, and made a substantive edit restore the revision without a human queue.
  • Added aggregate report and unlisting totals to the existing privacy-conscious site-health panel.

No reviews are deleted or rewritten by these controls. Ordinary community-ready reviews remain browseable, while private notes and reviews that do not meet a public tier remain in the owner's library.

September 2026: Activation and Trust Baseline

This release improves the first-session path and gives maintainers a privacy-conscious way to understand whether new accounts reach a useful library. It builds on the existing launchpad, Weekly playback, Library pulse, and moderator insights instead of creating duplicate onboarding or recap features.

  • Added aggregate stages for account creation, email verification, first library item, five saved titles, and a post-activation return.
  • Stored only the most recent successful login time and a running successful-login count; no page history, searches, media titles, or private notes are collected for the return signal.
  • Moved Google Analytics out of the private dashboard and limited it to the standalone public landing experience.
  • Made the public analytics loader honor supported Global Privacy Control and Do Not Track signals while disabling Google signals and ad-personalization signals.
  • Aligned account-deactivation language around the actual 90-day self-service reactivation window.
  • Consolidated stale development notes into the README, test guide, current content review, and one growth-operations reference.

The return measurement begins with this release and does not guess historical visits. AdSense approval still depends on Google's review and any required account-side consent configuration; these changes are a clearer operating baseline, not an approval guarantee.

September 2026: Recommendation Postcards

Recommendation Postcards turn the familiar “what should I try next?” conversation into a small, useful workflow without creating another public feed. Members can ask one focused question, choose eligible media types, and collect a limited number of thoughtful replies from friends or guests.

  • Added unlisted, high-entropy guest links with a 3-to-30-day reply window and a configurable response cap.
  • Added direct invitations for accepted OmniTrackr friends and one-response-per-friend protection.
  • Added a private owner inbox with save, add to library, add to Next Up, and dismiss decisions.
  • Kept all replies out of the library until the owner acts; matching library items retain their existing rating, review, metadata, and completion status.
  • Excluded guest pages and APIs from search indexing, caching, public profiles, and advertising inventory.
  • Added duplicate prevention, a spam-trap field, guest submission rate limits, category restrictions, and safe text rendering.

The database change is additive. Existing accounts, media records, reviews, collections, friendships, journal entries, and privacy settings are not rewritten or deleted.

September 2026: A Smaller, Stronger Public Library

After another low-value-content review, OmniTrackr narrowed its search and advertising footprint around the pages that offer the clearest standalone experience. No guides, accounts, collections, or library records were deleted. Supporting pages remain available through the site navigation, but the XML sitemap now recommends a smaller set of original product guides, examples, public reviews, and human-edited Discover pages.

  • Reduced the core sitemap to the public pages with the most distinct purpose, while preserving every existing route for visitors.
  • Marked overlapping category guides, product updates, comparison pages, and policy utilities noindex, follow so their links still help people without diluting search inventory.
  • Limited prospective ad loading to six substantial public experiences and kept it off supporting, legal, and noindex pages.
  • Kept member collections shareable by direct link but out of search and sitemap inventory until a moderation workflow exists.
  • Promoted the Discover index and authored monthly edition while treating shorter themed trails as supporting reading.
  • Verified authentication, accounts, media CRUD, privacy, friends, statistics, reviews, Discover, collections, and frontend security after the change.

This is a content-consolidation release, not a data migration. It changes how the public site presents itself to crawlers and advertising systems while leaving signed-in workflows and user-owned records unchanged.

September 2026: The Private Activity Journal

The Activity Journal gives a library a sense of time. Members can log when they start, progress through, finish, revisit, or simply make a note about any movie, TV show, anime, game, album, or book already in their library.

  • Added one chronological, private timeline across all six built-in media types.
  • Added a weekly playback with moment, completion, reflection, and category counts using the member's local calendar week.
  • Connected the existing finish-reflection flow to one automatic completion entry without changing the underlying media record.

  • Kept the journal out of public profiles, search indexing, and advertising inventory.

Existing items are intentionally not backfilled with guessed dates. The journal begins with real activity after the feature ships, while title and rating snapshots preserve useful context if a library entry changes later.

September 2026: Bring Your Library Import Studio

The Import Studio lowers the cost of trying OmniTrackr for people who already have years of media history elsewhere. It recognizes Letterboxd, Goodreads, MyAnimeList, and flexible spreadsheet exports while treating existing OmniTrackr records as the source of truth.

  • Added a no-write preview with ready, duplicate, and invalid outcomes before confirmation.
  • Added optional column mapping and downloadable templates for all six built-in media categories.
  • Kept migration imports additive: matching library records are skipped, never updated or deleted.
  • Bound confirmation to the exact file, format choice, category, and column mapping that produced the preview.
  • Made each confirmed batch atomic and kept imported reviews private by default.
  • Kept the established versioned JSON backup and restoration path backward compatible.

The migration API is authenticated, private-cache protected, and excluded from search indexing. No schema changes or existing-data migrations are required for this release.

September 2026: OmniTrackr Tasteprint

Tasteprint turns an established library into a concise portrait of how someone watches, plays, listens, and reads. It uses aggregate evidence from selected categories and the private activity journal without exposing media titles or review text through the Tasteprint API.

  • Added evidence-backed observations for media range, library anchor, completion style, rating personality, home decade, recurring creators, and recent journal rhythm.
  • Added a deliberate minimum-data threshold so new accounts see honest progress instead of invented conclusions.
  • Excluded hidden and private categories by default while allowing the account owner to opt them into a specific calculation.
  • Added per-insight controls, an optional username, a copied text summary, and a branded PNG rendered entirely in the browser.
  • Kept Tasteprint private, unindexed, uncached, ad-free, and separate from friend profiles.

No new public profile, database table, or stored share-card record is created. Downloading a card does not publish it; the user remains responsible for whether and where it is shared.

July 2026: Review Quality, Freshness, and Public Discovery

The July update continues the AdSense-readiness work by making the public website easier to evaluate, easier to browse, and less dependent on thin user-generated snippets. The goal is not to create more pages for their own sake; it is to make the existing media tracker clearer and more useful before someone signs up.

  • Added a landing-page "Recently improved" section that points new visitors and policy reviewers to the sample library, content quality policy, site map, and advertising transparency page.
  • Changed the public reviews API default to favor substantial reviews using the same review-length threshold used by the crawlable reviews page and XML sitemap.
  • Separated directory-quality reviews from standalone review detail pages so indexed review URLs require more complete review text before receiving sitemap or ad eligibility.
  • Raised the automated public ad-page content floor so eligible public pages must keep at least 750 crawlable words, structured data, one H1, multiple content sections, and canonical metadata.
  • Kept explicit broad review access available through min_chars=1 so existing public API callers can still request short opt-in notes without deleting or rewriting user content.
  • Documented public review quality expectations more clearly across the reviews page, review guidelines, content quality policy, and automated tests.
  • Verified that these changes do not modify account records, private libraries, ratings, friendships, imports, exports, or authenticated dashboard behavior.

This update is intentionally conservative. It improves what visitors and crawlers see on public pages while leaving the user-owned data model intact. It does not modify account records, private libraries, ratings, friendships, imports, exports, or dashboard workflows. Short personal notes still belong in private libraries; public discovery now defaults toward reviews that include enough context to help another person.

June 2026: Public Content, Privacy, and Ad Readiness

The June update focused on making OmniTrackr clearer to first-time visitors while protecting the existing app experience for signed-in users. The largest theme was turning the public website into a useful resource library instead of a thin shell around the login page.

  • Added new public guides for comparing media trackers and planning cross-media tracking workflows.
  • Added dedicated pages for TV show tracking and game tracking to give visitors useful information before signing up.

These changes do not alter existing user libraries, accounts, ratings, reviews, friendships, or privacy settings. They add public routes, templates, metadata, and tests around the edges of the app so the logged-in tracker can keep behaving the same way.

Recent Security and Quality Improvements

OmniTrackr recently received several hardening updates focused on safer rendering and dependency hygiene. The app removed the need for CSP unsafe-inline, improved nonce handling for public pages, replaced risky HTML rewriting patterns with parser-based handling, and upgraded dependency versions flagged by security scanning. These updates are important because a media tracker stores user-created libraries, reviews, account settings, and privacy preferences.

Security scanning feedback also led to more focused regression tests. The test suite now checks that public content pages use nonce-based scripts, that generated API documentation is noindexed, that the AdSense loader is limited to eligible public pages, and that review text is not reinterpreted as HTML in the browser.

Recent Content Improvements

The public side of OmniTrackr now includes more than a sign-in page. Visitors can read guides, compare tracking methods, learn how TV and game tracking workflows work, review privacy practices, and browse public reviews when users choose to share them. That public content helps people decide whether OmniTrackr fits their habits before they create an account.

Category guides were expanded around real decisions: movies need ratings and review context, TV shows need season and episode memory, anime often needs seasonal backlog notes, games need platform and completion tracking, music needs album and relisten context, and books need reading status plus author notes. This gives each page a reason to exist beyond repeating the same feature list.

Release Verification

Public-page changes are covered by automated tests for sitemap entries, robots.txt rules, page metadata, navigation links, content markers, CSP behavior, ad-loader behavior, public review filtering, and frontend safety checks. The purpose is simple: search and policy improvements should not break account registration, login, media CRUD operations, import/export, statistics, privacy controls, or friend features.

That verification matters because OmniTrackr is already live. A content update is only successful if it improves the public site while leaving user-created media collections and account data untouched.

What Is Next?

Planned improvements include more category-specific guides, clearer onboarding for new accounts, richer sample workflows, and continued dependency/security maintenance. The goal is to keep OmniTrackr useful for active users while making the public website easier for new visitors, search engines, and policy reviewers to understand.
