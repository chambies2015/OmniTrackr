OmniTrackr Roadmap

A public view of what recently improved, what is being refined now, and what is planned next for the media tracker.

Recently Shipped

OmniTrackr has moved beyond a simple private list. The latest release work focuses on making the public side useful before signup while keeping the logged-in tracker safe, fast, and familiar for existing users. Visitors can now read practical guides, compare tracking options, preview a sample library, understand privacy controls, and browse public reviews that users intentionally share.

Public Guide Library

Guides now explain movie, TV, anime, game, music, book, statistics, export, template, and review workflows in crawlable pages.

Automated Review Quality and Safety

Community and search-ready tiers now share one quality engine, while signed-browser reports can temporarily unlist an exact review version without deleting it or creating a manual queue.

Automated Collection Discovery

Published collections now use one readiness result across the editor, gallery, search metadata, and sitemap, with version-bound reports and self-service recovery instead of a human queue.

Ad and Privacy Transparency

Advertising and privacy pages explain where ads may appear, how Google ads use cookies, and why private account areas stay separate.

Security and SEO Cleanup

Recent work tightened CSP handling, removed unsafe inline requirements, improved metadata, and kept generated API docs out of search results.

Activation and Trust Baseline

The existing moderator health view now shows aggregate first-session milestones, while analytics stays out of the private dashboard.

Welcome Back Deck

Activated members returning after a real absence now get one private, dismissible route back into Next Up, unfinished titles, reflections, and recent journal activity.

Now

Current priorities are deliberately practical. The goal is to learn from the new activation baseline, finish first-session reliability work, and preserve the workflows existing users already rely on.

  • Monitor the path from verified account to first title, five saved titles, and a later return.
  • Reduce save and import friction where the aggregate milestones show a meaningful drop.
  • Keep account, privacy, export, and advertising language aligned with production behavior.
  • Keep ads limited to public informational pages so the private tracking dashboard does not become cluttered.

Current work also emphasizes content quality. Public pages should answer real tracking questions instead of acting as empty search pages. Category guides, review guidance, comparison pages, and demo content are being expanded so visitors can learn something useful even before they decide to register.

Next

The Welcome Back candidate has shipped by composing existing dashboard signals rather than creating another workflow. The next feature decision will follow its anonymous aggregate use alongside the established activation path.

  • Refine the existing import templates and launchpad where first-session evidence shows friction.
  • Improve statistics with trends, rating coverage, backlog progress, and clearer category comparisons.
  • Measure search-ready review and collection coverage plus automated-report health before expanding public discovery or advertising inventory.

Later

Longer-term ideas stay focused on portability, control, and useful context. OmniTrackr should help users remember why something mattered, not just store a title and rating.

  • More flexible recommendation notes, including who recommended an item and why it was added.
  • More public profile controls for people who want to share selected lists safely.
  • Optional richer metadata for custom tabs while preserving exportability and privacy.
  • More ways to summarize a year of watching, reading, playing, and listening across all categories.

Stability and Release Guardrails

Because OmniTrackr is already live, roadmap work has to protect existing accounts and media libraries. Public-content updates should not require destructive migrations, reset user settings, remove saved entries, or change private/public review decisions without the user's intent.

Recent verification covers account flows, authentication, CRUD endpoints for each media category, import/export, friend and privacy behavior, statistics, public reviews, SEO routes, static security checks, and content-security-policy behavior. That testing focus keeps growth work tied to the real product instead of only the marketing pages.

How Roadmap Items Are Chosen

Roadmap ideas are evaluated against two questions: will this make everyday tracking easier, and can it ship without putting existing user data at risk? A feature that looks impressive but complicates privacy, exports, or account stability is less valuable than a smaller improvement that users can trust immediately.

Public-page work follows the same rule. A new guide should answer a real tracking question, link to related resources, and stay useful even if the visitor never creates an account. That keeps the site from becoming a collection of empty search pages and gives returning users a clearer place to find product changes.

For the current release, that means prioritizing review quality, stronger public examples, clearer privacy and advertising disclosures, and regression tests that protect the live tracker while the public content surface grows.

Why Publish This?

A roadmap helps users see that OmniTrackr is maintained and gives new visitors a reason to come back. It also explains the product direction: a practical, privacy-aware tracker for many kinds of media rather than a thin single-purpose list. That matters for people deciding whether to trust a new tracker with their history, ratings, reviews, and private notes.

The roadmap is not a promise that every idea will ship in exactly this order. It is a transparent product note about what the app is optimizing for: useful public resources, portable user data, safer sharing, stronger review quality, and a calmer advertising approach.
