About OmniTrackr

A free, independent tracker for everything you watch, play, read and hear, and a library of free guides to what to try next.

What OmniTrackr is

OmniTrackr keeps movies, TV shows, anime, video games, albums and books in one private library. You can rate them, write notes and reviews, save the episode or page you stopped at, and see how your taste changes over time. It runs in the browser on any device, it is free, and you can export everything whenever you like.

It exists because media habits rarely stay inside one app. A streaming service remembers its own catalog, a storefront remembers purchases, and a notes app holds loose recommendations from friends. None of them gives you the whole picture: what you meant to try, what you finished, what you loved, what you dropped and what you would hand to a friend. OmniTrackr is built around that whole picture.

What you can read without an account

Most of the public site is written for people who will never sign up, and that is fine. Everything below is free to read:

  • In order guides walk through long franchises such as Star Wars, The Witcher, Dune and Zelda. Each gives more than one order, says who each one suits and recommends one.
  • If you liked guides start from one favourite and suggest what to try next in other formats, with a caveat for every pick.
  • Discover guides connect a film, a show, a game, an album and a book through one idea, compare them by pace and commitment, and link the sources used to check the facts. A monthly edition on the Discover page adds a new theme each month.
  • OmniTrackr Daily is a guess-the-title puzzle with a new movie, show, anime, game, book or album every day. Past puzzles stay playable, with the answer and a short note on why the title is worth your time.
  • Release Radar lists what comes out this month, and the how-to guides explain tracking, importing from other services and backing up a library. If you are choosing a tracker, our comparison sets OmniTrackr next to IMDb, Letterboxd, Trakt and Goodreads and says where each one is the better fit.

Who runs it

OmniTrackr is an independent project run by one person. It is not part of a studio, publisher, streaming service or media company, and nobody pays to be included in a guide or to be ranked higher in one.

The guides are written with AI assistance and reviewed by the site owner before they are published. Release years, cast, creators and story order are checked against public and official sources, and the Discover guides link every source they use. Opinions about mood, pace and who a title suits are labelled as editorial opinion, never presented as facts or as first-hand experience. Every guide shows the date it was last checked. The editorial standards page explains the rules in full.

If something is wrong, out of date or spoils more than it should, tell us. Corrections are made on the page itself and the checked date moves forward.

How it is paid for

OmniTrackr has no paywall: every tracking feature is free and stays free. Hosting is paid for in two ways: advertising on some public guide pages, and readers who choose to support the site on Ko-fi. Signed-in members do not see ads, and ads never appear inside a private library. The ads and affiliates page lists where ads can appear and how affiliate links are labelled, and the supporters page thanks the people who chip in.

Your library stays yours

Libraries are private by default. A review becomes public only when you tick Public Review on that one entry, and you choose separately what friends can see in each category. Your personal information is not sold, and nothing in your library is shared with advertisers; the privacy policy has the detail.

You can export your library and journal as a file at any time and import it again later, so years of history are never trapped here. If you are coming from another service, the switching guide shows how to bring your lists with you.

How it changes

OmniTrackr is updated most weeks. What's new lists every release, and the roadmap says what is being considered next. Updates are additive: a new feature never removes or rewrites what is already in your library.
